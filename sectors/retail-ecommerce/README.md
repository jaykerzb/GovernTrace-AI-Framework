# Sector Pack: Retail / E-commerce

For consumer-facing retail and e-commerce organizations using AI for personalization, recommendations, dynamic pricing, chatbots, or marketing. Layer this on top of the core [Risk Assessment](../../templates/ai-risk-assessment.md) and [Pre-Deployment Checklist](../../checklists/pre-deployment-checklist.md) — see the [sector pack overview](../README.md).

## Why retail/e-commerce needs its own overlay

This sector has less regulatory depth than banking or healthcare, but higher **volume and consumer-protection exposure**: AI here touches nearly every customer, runs at scale, and is squarely in FTC/consumer-protection enforcement territory — especially around pricing fairness, dark patterns, and deceptive AI-driven claims.

## Key regulatory context

| Regime | Relevance |
|---|---|
| **FTC Act (Section 5 — unfair or deceptive practices)** | The FTC has explicitly targeted AI-driven dark patterns, deceptive AI marketing claims ("AI-washing"), and algorithmic pricing/discrimination — the primary federal exposure for this sector. |
| **State consumer protection / UDAP laws** | State-level equivalents to the FTC Act, often with a private right of action — meaning consumers (not just regulators) can sue. |
| **State privacy laws** (CCPA/CPRA and similar) | Govern use of consumer data for personalization/profiling, including opt-out rights for certain automated decision-making and profiling. |
| **Automated/algorithmic pricing scrutiny** | Growing regulatory attention (FTC and some state AGs) on AI-driven dynamic and personalized pricing, particularly where it could function as price discrimination or algorithmic collusion in shared-vendor pricing tools. |
| **Advertising substantiation rules** | Claims made or generated by AI in marketing content still need to meet the same substantiation standards as human-written claims. |

## Additional risk categories for the Risk Assessment

Add to [AI Risk Assessment](../../templates/ai-risk-assessment.md) §3:

- [ ] **Dark pattern risk** — could this system's design (urgency prompts, personalized nudges, AI-driven upsells) cross from personalization into manipulation under FTC scrutiny?
- [ ] **Pricing fairness** — if using dynamic/personalized pricing, could this result in discriminatory pricing across protected classes or geographies, even unintentionally via proxy variables?
- [ ] **AI-washing / deceptive marketing claims** — are claims about "AI-powered" features accurate, or could they mislead consumers about capability?
- [ ] **Chatbot accuracy and liability** — for AI customer service, could hallucinated answers (pricing, policy terms, availability) create liability if a customer reasonably relies on them?
- [ ] **Profiling opt-out compliance** — does the system respect applicable state privacy law opt-outs for automated profiling/targeted advertising?

## Additional Pre-Deployment Checklist items

- [ ] Marketing/legal review of any AI-related claims made to consumers ("AI-powered," "personalized for you," etc.) for accuracy
- [ ] Dynamic/personalized pricing logic reviewed for disparate impact across protected classes and geography
- [ ] Chatbot/customer-service AI tested for hallucination risk on high-stakes topics (pricing, returns, legal terms) with clear escalation to a human
- [ ] Consumer opt-out mechanisms for automated profiling/targeted advertising implemented and tested where required by applicable state law
- [ ] Dark-pattern review: would a reasonable consumer feel misled or manipulated by this system's design?

## Sector-specific terms

- **Dark pattern** — a UI/UX design (increasingly AI-personalized) that manipulates users into decisions they wouldn't otherwise make; explicit FTC enforcement focus.
- **Algorithmic pricing** — use of AI/algorithms to set or adjust prices dynamically, including personalized pricing to individual consumers based on their data.
- **AI-washing** — marketing a product as "AI-powered" or more capable than it actually is; an FTC enforcement theory under deceptive practices law.
